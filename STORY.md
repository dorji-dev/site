# Dorji Tshering — Developer Story

> One-line version: Anyone can learn anything — I learned between wild animals and slow Wi‑Fi.

- I am a self-taught developer from Bhutan.

---

## High School

- Studied at Tashitse Higher Secondary School, Tashigang, Bhutan.
- Graduated from class 12 in 2016 as the school topper and had earned 4 HM certificates by then.
- I enjoyed physics and math classes.
- Had no idea what coding even meant.
- I wanted to be a cosmologist. I would spend time pondering existential crises and would leave my friends feeling hopeless when I told them things like, "What if one day an external force wipes out the Earth?" If that happened, nothing—not the present, not the future—seemed to matter.

## College

- Got a government scholarship and took B.Tech mechanical engineering at NIT Kurukshetra, India, starting in mid-2017.
- I loved the college environment and had lots of fun. I was on the college football team and went to Goa for the Inter-NIT football tournament.

## Dropout & The Pivot

- Dropped out in 2019 due to a psychological condition. I was in a new place, enjoyed it initially, but something crept in like a wave of utter hopelessness.
- Came back to Bhutan; my dearest college friends supported my airfare.
- I just wanted to take a break and figure out what to do next.
- I was ready to take my next step after some time but still couldn't figure out what to do. Then the National Day speech by His Majesty the King of Bhutan on 17 December 2019 inspired me to venture into coding. I knew I could take that path since I was a topper in high school and I had it in me to take on coding and software development.

## Choosing Coding

- Started with C in early 2020. I tried to solve problems I couldn't during college, like matrix multiplication—which, looking back, was just an array operation with for loops and if statements, haha. Then I moved on to HTML and CSS.
- My first resources were programming notes and online PDF series like the Head First series. The series was so easy to understand, and I learned Java, a little bit of C++, HTML & CSS, and JavaScript.
- My proudest early moment was when I replicated a Bhutan yearly calendar design with HTML and CSS. I loved it and shared it in a Bhutanese Facebook group.
- I was so into it that I had never enjoyed learning this much—not even during my school or college days.
- With slow internet, I learned coding for about 5 months starting from May 2020 till September in my village. I would spend the whole day in a makeshift shelter in the middle of a corn field, as I had to protect the field from wild animals and learn coding at the same time on a Windows laptop (I use a Mac now).

## 2020-2023

- Still learning.
- Applying for jobs here and there.
- Attending IT workshops whenever I could.

## First Job

- I got my first job at Selise Bhutan in April 2023 through an online coding test with three problems rated easy, medium, and hard. I solved the easy and medium problems but couldn't finish the hard one in time, so I left a code comment saying, "Couldn't finish due to time constraint." That comment is how I got the interview—apparently other applicants had seemingly used AI to solve all the problems. Lucky me.
- I got to work on my first professional React project. Then I led the frontend for the revamp of an internal portal for Swiss Migros Group and completed it seamlessly using Angular 19. That was also the first time I learned Angular on the go; the switch was easy since I already had a solid grasp of frontend logic through React.
- Personally, since a coding problem can have multiple solutions, it has helped me see everyday problems from different angles and make sounder judgments—in work and in life.

## Second Job (Jaggle.AI)

- It's a startup founded by someone I know, and he wanted me to join, so I left my old job and joined them in October 2025. On the personal side, I was intrigued by the risk—the startup could fail—but I liked the idea of building something and watching it succeed.
- Currently, I work as a frontend developer for the startup product, which is a GNH-guided project management platform.
- I am growing into a more significant role here, preferably as frontend lead for the product. I am responsible for code quality, reviewing PRs, pointing out code and pattern mistakes, keeping track of open-source libraries we use to avoid license violations, and updating the team on the tech landscape.
- I custom-built a high-performance Gantt chart as well, since the existing libraries didn't meet our requirements.

## What I Love About Development

- I love clean code that does its job. No bloated code (especially now that we have AI), and I love playing with new language/framework APIs and features as soon as they become stable.
- I love the feeling when the code I shipped actually powers end users without any issues.
- I have always loved custom implementations of libraries if their features don't meet what I have in mind.

- Serious at work and a regularly funny, positive friend outside. I love cracking jokes (mental health jokes are fair game now; I'm well past that chapter and mentally strong).
